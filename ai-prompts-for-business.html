💼 Best AI Prompts for Business Growth

15 proven prompts to accelerate your business with AI

Why Business Professionals Need AI Prompts

The business world moves fast, and those who leverage AI effectively are gaining a serious competitive edge. AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini can draft sales emails, analyze markets, create business plans, and generate persuasive copy in seconds — tasks that used to take hours. But the quality of what you get back depends entirely on what you put in. A vague request yields a generic, forgettable response. A well-crafted prompt yields actionable, targeted output you can use immediately.

Whether you're a solopreneur, a startup founder, or part of a corporate team, mastering AI prompts transforms you from someone who "uses AI" into someone who commands it. The prompts below cover the most common and high-impact business tasks — from writing cold emails that convert to building full business plans and running competitor analysis — all with real examples of what to expect.

1. Generate a High-Converting Sales Email

What This Does: Creates a persuasive sales email personalized for your product, audience, and goal — ready to send or tweak.

You are an expert email copywriter. Write a high-converting sales email for [PRODUCT/SERVICE]. The target audience is [AUDIENCE]. The email should: - Have a compelling subject line that boosts open rates - Open with a pain point the reader relates to - Present [PRODUCT/SERVICE] as the solution - Include 3 key benefits with brief explanations - End with a clear, urgent call-to-action - Tone: professional but conversational - Length: 200-300 words

📝 Example Output:

Subject: Still spending 4 hours on reports that should take 20 minutes?

Hi [Name],

If you're like most small business owners, you're drowning in manual tasks that eat into your actual money-making hours...

That's exactly why we built AutoFlow Pro — a simple automation tool designed for non-technical teams...

→ Try it free for 14 days. No credit card required. Start now →

2. Create a 90-Day Marketing Strategy

What This Does: Generates a structured, actionable marketing plan with monthly milestones and specific tactics.

Act as a senior marketing strategist. Create a detailed 90-day marketing strategy for a [TYPE OF BUSINESS] targeting [AUDIENCE]. Include: Month 1 — Foundation: - Brand positioning and messaging framework - Content pillars (3-5 themes) - Platform selection with justification Month 2 — Growth: - Content calendar highlights - Paid advertising recommendations (budget: [BUDGET]) - Partnership/collaboration ideas Month 3 — Optimization: - KPIs to track and benchmark goals - A/B testing priorities - Scaling tactics for what's working Format as a clear, actionable plan with bullet points.

📝 Example Output:

Month 1 — Foundation: Position your AI consulting firm as the "no-jargon AI partner for SMBs." Content pillars: AI myths debunked, case studies, quick-win tutorials. Focus on LinkedIn + newsletter...

Month 2 — Growth: Launch LinkedIn ad campaign ($500/mo) targeting ops managers. Publish 2 case studies. Partner with a complementary SaaS for co-webinar...

4. Build a Lean Business Plan

What This Does: Creates a concise but comprehensive one-page business plan you can use for planning, pitching, or securing funding.

Act as a business consultant with 20 years of experience. Create a lean one-page business plan for a [BUSINESS IDEA]. Include: - Problem: What pain point does this solve? - Solution: How does your product/service solve it? - Target Market: Who are your ideal customers? (demographics + psychographics) - Revenue Model: How will you make money? (pricing, tiers, upsells) - Unique Value Proposition: What makes you different from competitors? - Key Metrics: 5 KPIs to track in the first 6 months - Marketing Channels: Top 3 channels to acquire customers - Cost Structure: Estimated monthly expenses breakdown - 6-Month Revenue Projection: Realistic estimate with assumptions Keep it concise and actionable. Use bullet points.

📝 Example Output:

Problem: Freelancers waste 5+ hours/week creating invoices and chasing payments manually.

Solution: InvoiceBot — AI-powered invoicing that auto-generates, sends, and follows up on invoices.

Revenue Model: Freemium ($0 for 5 invoices/mo), Pro ($12/mo), Business ($29/mo)...

5. Craft a Customer Re-Engagement Campaign

What This Does: Creates a multi-touch campaign to win back inactive customers and boost retention.

You are a customer retention specialist. Design a 3-email re-engagement sequence for [BUSINESS] targeting customers who haven't purchased in 60+ days. For each email include: - Subject line (optimized for open rates) - Preview text - Email body (150-250 words) - Call-to-action - Timing (days between emails) Strategy requirements: - Email 1: "We miss you" — emotional reconnection + value reminder - Email 2: Exclusive offer or incentive to return - Email 3: Final nudge with urgency/scarcity Tone: warm but not desperate. Include personalization placeholders like [FIRST_NAME] and [LAST_PURCHASE].

📝 Example Output:

Email 1 (Day 0) — Subject: "We noticed you've been away, [FIRST_NAME] 👋"

Hey [FIRST_NAME], it's been a while since your last order ([LAST_PURCHASE]). We've been busy adding new features we think you'll love...

Email 2 (Day 4) — Subject: "A little something just for you — 20% off"

Email 3 (Day 8) — Subject: "Last chance: your 20% discount expires tonight"

6. Market Research Snapshot

What This Does: Generates a comprehensive market overview for any industry or niche, including size, trends, and customer insights — in minutes instead of days.

Act as a market research analyst. Give me a comprehensive market snapshot for the [INDUSTRY/NICHE] market. Include: - Market size (global and regional, if known) - Top 3 market trends shaping the industry in 2026 - Primary customer segments and their key pain points - Top 5 competitors and their positioning - Biggest market opportunities (gaps or underserved niches) - Key risks or threats to entering this market - Recommended go-to-market angle for a new entrant Provide data-backed insights where possible. Format with clear headers.

📝 Example Output:

Market: AI-powered productivity tools for remote teams

Size: $12.5B globally, growing at 23% CAGR through 2027

Top Trends: Async-first workflows, AI meeting summaries, integration with Slack/Notion ecosystems...

Biggest Opportunity: SMBs with 10-50 employees are underserved by enterprise tools — high willingness to pay for simple, one-click solutions.

7. Competitor Analysis Framework

What This Does: Produces a detailed competitive breakdown to help you understand your rivals' strengths, weaknesses, and where you can win.

Act as a competitive intelligence strategist. Analyze [COMPETITOR NAME] as a competitor to my [TYPE OF BUSINESS]. Cover: 1. Their core product/service offering and pricing model 2. Their target customer and positioning statement 3. Strengths (what they do well) 4. Weaknesses (where they fall short — look at reviews, complaints, gaps) 5. Their marketing strategy (content, SEO, ads, social) 6. Key differentiators that set them apart 7. Vulnerabilities I can exploit as a competitor 8. Recommended strategy to compete with or position against them Be objective and strategic.

📝 Example Output:

Competitor: Jasper AI vs. my AI writing tool startup

Weaknesses: Steep learning curve, expensive for solopreneurs ($49+/mo), limited template customization at base tier...

My Opportunity: Target freelancers and small creators with a simpler UX at $19/mo with unlimited generations.

8. Cold Outreach Email Sequence

What This Does: Creates a 3-touch cold email sequence that opens conversations, builds interest, and books meetings — without feeling pushy.

You are an expert B2B sales copywriter. Write a 3-email cold outreach sequence for [MY BUSINESS] targeting [PROSPECT TYPE]. Email 1 — The Hook (Day 1): - Subject line that piques curiosity (not clickbait) - Personalized opener mentioning something specific about their business - One clear pain point they likely have - Brief introduction to how I help - Soft CTA (a question, not a pitch) Email 2 — The Value Add (Day 4): - Reference the first email naturally - Share a quick win, tip, or case study relevant to them - One sentence about what we do - CTA: ask if it's worth a 15-minute call Email 3 — The Breakup (Day 9): - Light, friendly tone - Acknowledge they're busy - Final offer of value - Leave the door open Each email: under 150 words. Tone: direct but human.

📝 Example Output:

Email 1 Subject: "Quick question about [Company]'s onboarding process"

Hi [Name], I noticed [Company] recently expanded to [City] — congrats on the growth. Companies scaling quickly often hit a wall when onboarding new hires manually...

Email 3 Subject: "Closing the loop, [Name]"

Hey [Name], I'll stop nudging — just wanted to leave you with this one resource that our clients find useful: [RESOURCE]. If timing ever works out, my calendar is open. — [Your Name]

9. SWOT Analysis Generator

What This Does: Produces a thorough SWOT analysis for any business, product, or strategic decision — useful for planning, pitching, or quarterly reviews.

Act as a strategic business consultant. Conduct a comprehensive SWOT analysis for [BUSINESS/PRODUCT/DECISION]. Background: - Business type: [DESCRIPTION] - Stage: [Early-stage / Growth / Mature] - Market: [TARGET MARKET] - Main challenge: [CURRENT PROBLEM OR GOAL] Provide: - Strengths: Internal advantages (at least 5) - Weaknesses: Internal limitations (at least 5) - Opportunities: External trends or gaps to exploit (at least 5) - Threats: External risks or competitive pressures (at least 5) After the SWOT, provide: - Top 3 SO Strategies (using Strengths to capture Opportunities) - Top 2 WT Strategies (addressing Weaknesses to reduce Threats) Be honest and specific — not generic.

📝 Example Output:

Strengths: Proprietary algorithm, founder has 10 years domain expertise, no-code product reduces customer friction...

Opportunities: Growing demand for AI automation in SMBs, competitors underserving the healthcare vertical...

SO Strategy: Launch a healthcare-specific product tier by Q3, leveraging existing algorithm + founder's medical admin background.

Refund request (policy allows partial refunds) 2. Product/service complaint — issue is resolved 3. Delayed order or delivery 4. Account cancellation — with retention offer 5. Billing error — customer was overcharged For each template include: - Subject line - Opening that acknowledges their frustration - Clear explanation or resolution - Next step or CTA - Warm, professional sign-off Tone: [BRAND TONE — e.g., friendly and professional / formal and courteous] Brand name: [YOUR BRAND]

Hi [Name], Thank you for reaching out. We completely understand your frustration, and we want to make this right. Per our policy, you're eligible for a partial refund of [AMOUNT]...

Retention Offer: Before you go, we'd love to offer you 2 months free to explore our new [FEATURE] — many customers who've had similar concerns found it solved their problem entirely.

11. Meeting Agenda and Summary Creator

What This Does: Generates structured pre-meeting agendas that keep discussions on track, and transforms raw notes into clean meeting summaries with action items.

You are an experienced project manager. Help me with two tasks: TASK 1 — Create a meeting agenda: Meeting type: [MEETING TYPE — e.g., weekly team standup / client strategy review / board update] Duration: [X minutes] Goal: [WHAT MUST BE DECIDED OR ACCOMPLISHED] Attendees: [LIST ROLES] Key topics to cover: [LIST 3-5 TOPICS] Format: Time-blocked agenda with owner for each section. TASK 2 — Summarize meeting notes: [PASTE MEETING NOTES OR TRANSCRIPT] Produce: 1. 3-sentence summary 2. Key decisions made (bulleted) 3. Action items table (Task | Owner | Due Date | Priority) 4. Open questions for next meeting

📝 Example Output — Agenda:

0:00–0:05 — Welcome & objectives (Host)

0:05–0:20 — Q2 pipeline review (Sales Lead)

0:20–0:35 — Product roadmap update (CTO)

0:35–0:45 — Budget discussion (CFO)

0:45–0:55 — Open decisions & blockers (All)

0:55–1:00 — Action items & wrap-up (Host)

Post requirements: - Hook: First line must stop the scroll — use a bold statement, surprising fact, or contrarian take - No fluff — every line should add value or advance the story - Use short paragraphs (1-2 sentences max) for mobile readability - Include a personal story or specific experience (I'll provide: [YOUR STORY/EXAMPLE]) - Share 3-5 actionable insights or lessons - End with a question to drive comments - Tone: [Authentic and direct / Professional and inspiring / Bold and contrarian] - Length: 150-300 words - DO NOT use generic phrases like "excited to share" or "humbled to announce"

14. Performance Review Summary Writer

What This Does: Transforms rough notes about an employee's performance into professional, balanced, and legally sound performance review language.

You are an experienced HR professional and manager. Help me write a professional performance review for [EMPLOYEE NAME/ROLE]. My rough notes: - Strengths: [LIST WHAT THEY DID WELL] - Areas for improvement: [LIST CHALLENGES OR GAPS] - Key achievements this period: [LIST 2-3 WINS] - Goals for next period: [LIST 2-3 GOALS] - Overall rating: [EXCEEDS / MEETS / BELOW EXPECTATIONS] Please write: 1. Opening summary paragraph (2-3 sentences) 2. Strengths section with specific examples (3 points) 3. Development areas section — framed constructively, not critically (2-3 points) 4. Goals for next review period (SMART format) 5. Closing paragraph that's encouraging and forward-looking Tone: Professional, fair, and growth-oriented. Avoid vague language.

📝 Example Output:

Opening: "Sarah consistently demonstrated strong initiative and technical proficiency throughout Q1-Q2. Her work on the platform migration project was a standout contribution that directly reduced customer churn by 12%."

Development Area (Framed Constructively): "To maximize her impact in senior roles, Sarah would benefit from developing her stakeholder communication skills — particularly in presenting technical findings to non-technical audiences. We'll support this with a presentation skills workshop in Q3."

15. Business Plan Executive Summary

What This Does: Generates a concise, investor-ready executive summary for any business concept — the most important page of any business plan.

Act as a startup advisor who has helped raise over $50M in funding. Write a compelling one-page executive summary for the following business: Business concept: [DESCRIPTION] Problem being solved: [SPECIFIC PAIN POINT] Target market: [WHO AND HOW LARGE] Revenue model: [HOW YOU MAKE MONEY] Traction (if any): [USERS, REVENUE, PARTNERSHIPS, ETC.] Competitive advantage: [WHAT MAKES YOU UNIQUE] Funding ask (if applicable): [AMOUNT AND USE OF FUNDS] Team: [KEY MEMBERS AND RELEVANT EXPERIENCE] Write a 400-500 word executive summary with: - Opening hook that captures the opportunity - Problem and solution statement - Market size (TAM/SAM/SOM) - Business model and unit economics - Traction and milestones - Team credentials - Closing ask or vision statement Make it compelling, specific, and investor-ready.

📝 Example Output:

Opening: "Healthcare providers waste 6.8 million hours annually on manual prior authorization paperwork — a bureaucratic bottleneck that delays patient care and burns out clinical staff. MedClear automates this process end-to-end with AI, reducing authorization time from 3 days to under 4 hours."

Market: $2.3B prior authorization software market growing at 18% CAGR. MedClear targets the 45,000 US physician group practices as initial SAM ($680M).

🎯 How to Customize These Prompts for Your Industry

The 15 prompts above are designed as adaptable templates. The key to unlocking their full power is replacing the placeholder variables with highly specific information about your business, industry, and target customer.

For example, if you run a healthcare clinic, replace [BUSINESS] with "a family medicine practice serving suburban communities" and [AUDIENCE] with "patients aged 35–65 managing chronic conditions." The more specific your context, the more targeted and useful the AI's output will be.

Another powerful technique is prompt chaining — using the output from one prompt as input for the next. For instance, generate a customer persona first (Prompt 11), then feed that persona directly into your sales email prompt (Prompt 1) or landing page copy prompt (Prompt 12). The result is deeply cohesive, audience-specific content that reads nothing like generic AI output.

You can also ask the AI to critique its own output: after generating a sales email, add "Now review this email and identify 3 ways to make it more persuasive for a skeptical buyer." This iterative approach consistently produces professional-grade results that rival those of experienced copywriters and consultants. Save your best prompt variations as templates so you can reuse and refine them over time.
